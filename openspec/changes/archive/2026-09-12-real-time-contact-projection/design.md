# Design: real-time-contact-projection (measured, not adopted)

## The correction

For a contact with penetration depth `pen >= 0` and constraint gradients `J_i`
(`i` over the participating vertices), the mass-weighted least-squares
correction that drives `pen` to zero is

```
W      = sum_i w_i |J_i|^2 ,      w_i = 1 / m_i
lambda = omega * pen / W ,        0 <= omega <= 1
dx_i   = w_i J_i^T lambda
```

Vertex-face (`normal` points towards the vertex, `u,v,w` barycentric weights of
the closest point):

```
J_v = n ,  J_{f,i} = -beta_i n
W   = w_v + (u^2 + v^2 + w^2) w_f
```

Edge-edge (the engine's `compute_edge_edge_contact` returns `s` on edge 1 and
`t` on edge 2, with the normal pushing edge 1 along `+n`):

```
J_p0 = (1-s) n ,  J_p1 = s n ,  J_q0 = -(1-t) n ,  J_q1 = -t n
W    = w_p0 (1-s)^2 + w_p1 s^2 + w_q0 (1-t)^2 + w_q1 t^2
```

Because `d(pen)` = `lambda * W`, one sweep with `omega = 1` lands exactly on
the manifold and `omega < 1` under-relaxes; the correction can never overshoot
the contact it came from.

## The safety bounds (as implemented and measured)

Four independent bounds, all of them required by the product statement that
penetration is inevitable:

1. `lambda` is clamped to `[0, ...]`, so a contact can only ever *reduce*
   penetration, never pull two pieces of cloth together.
2. `lambda` is additionally capped by `max_step / max_i w_i`, so one contact
   cannot move a vertex further than `max_step` in a single sweep.
3. The accumulated per-vertex correction is capped at `max_step` again when it
   is applied, which bounds the sum of a whole sweep (many contacts acting on
   one vertex, or a vertex wedged between two layers). `max_step` defaults to
   `query_radius`, the radius the candidate set was built with.
4. The projection runs *before* the velocity step, so the substep velocity is
   recomputed from the corrected positions and still passes through the
   existing `max_vel` clamp and the ground projection. A correction therefore
   cannot turn into an unbounded velocity kick.

An infeasible configuration (a vertex fully inside a body, cloth pinched
between two layers, a degenerate normal) leaves a residual penetration that is
simply carried to the next substep; the per-substep movement stays bounded, so
the worst case is slow progress, not a blow-up.

Obstacles and pinned vertices have zero weight, so they never move: the cloth
slides out along the normal instead of pushing the body.

### Measured failure of the first bound set

The first implementation used the two-sided mass-weighted solve with `max_step`
defaulting to `query_radius` (2 mm). On the two-layer stack that produced a
6.7 mm per substep blow-up: each vertex has many candidate contacts, each of
them a legitimate (if shallow) penetration, and their corrections summed to
the cap every sweep. This is the failure mode the fourth bound was written for
and it was real, not hypothetical.

The second and third implementations removed it:

* one-sided "deepest contact per primitive" (a vertex only receives its own
  push, never the neighbouring primitives' reactions), and
* the accumulated per-vertex correction bounded by `omega * (that vertex's own
  deepest penetration)`, plus a `contact_projection_threshold` so the penalty
  contacts keep their own small equilibrium penetration.

Both were stable (finite, bounded) but neither reduced the jitter: the stack
stayed at 6.2 mm with the threshold disabled and 0.9-1.0 mm with it enabled,
against a 0.78-0.80 mm baseline, and the 3 mm layer went from 0.59 mm to 4.7 mm
(most aggressive) or 0.69 mm (most conservative).

## Why the mechanism is not a penetration residual

`self_contact_probe.py` samples the engine's own candidate lists and narrow
phase after the scene settles:

| Scene | candidates per vertex | valid cross contacts | valid self contacts | median penetration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat single layer, 8 mm | 1.24 | 0 | 0 | - |
| flat single layer, 3 mm | 13.80 | 0 | 0 | - |
| two flat layers, 8 mm | 8.39 | ~1 per sampled vertex | 0 | 7.9 um |

The penetration scale is therefore micrometres while the per-substep motion is
0.6-0.8 mm. The oscillation is generated by the contact *force* response (the
contact forces are coupled but their operator contribution is diagonal only),
not by leftover overlap, so removing overlap cannot damp it: the only
corrections large enough to matter are larger than the penetration itself,
which is precisely what destabilises the loop.

The ablations agree: without contact forces the jitter disappears entirely,
with softer contacts it shrinks in proportion to the stiffness, and neither
iteration counts, substep size nor global damping changes it.

## Placement in the substep

`SolverPDNewton::step` runs the PD loop (optionally as a captured CUDA graph),
joins the work stream, and then calls `step_end_kernel` which derives the
velocity from the position difference, clamps `|v| <= max_vel` and applies the
ground projection. The contact projection is inserted between the join and
`step_end_kernel`, after `linear->consume_failure_flag()`. That ordering is
what gives bound 4 above, and it also means the projection is outside the
captured region, so no graph key has to change.

The candidate lists are the ones the substep's broad phase produced (inflated
by `query_radius`), which is why `max_step` defaults to that radius: the
projection can only use contacts that the broad phase still covers.

## Alternatives

**Couple the contact Hessian into `Jx_nondiag`.** Keeps the current penalty
semantics, but the operator row space has to grow by the contact set, and the
narrow phase must not be re-run per matvec (the candidate scan costs about
0.1 ms per pass, i.e. ~6 ms per frame at 60 matvecs). Measured marginal matvec
cost on this GPU is 1.45 ns per row (plus 29 us of fixed per-iteration launch
and reduction overhead, from a `linear_iters` 2 -> 20 sweep). With four
gathers and four atomics per contact row (about 2x an edge row) and the
measured row budgets:

| Scene | operator rows today | vf+ee candidates | ratio | added frame cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat single layer, 8 mm | 16,644 | 3,495 | 0.21x | +7% |
| flat single layer, 3 mm | 29,078 | 71,306 | 2.45x | +107% |
| two flat layers, 8 mm | 33,288 | 167,629 | 5.04x | +247% |

(Candidate counts are an upper bound; a compaction that keeps only validated
contacts scales the ratio down by the validation rate. The `ef` candidates
belong to `solve_untangling_kernel` and are not part of the elastic operator.)

Combined with the measurements above, this is the only option that addresses
the actual mechanism at unchanged contact stiffness. It stays out of the
shipped change because of its cost, but it is the recommended direction if the
jitter must be removed without softening the contacts.

**Reduce the contact stiffness.** `ee_force_k`/`vf_force_k` 0.5 -> 0.1 takes
the two-layer stack from 0.81 mm to 0.12 mm and the 3 mm layer from 0.64 mm to
0.13 mm (5-6x) at the cost of deeper resting penetration. This is a parameter
change available to users today, and it is the cheapest mitigation found.

## Implemented and measured: contact rows in the operator

The narrow phase was extended to also emit one compact row per validated
contact - four vertex ids, the four coefficients of the gap gradient `J`, the
contact normal and its stiffness - and `A_mul_x` applies the rank-one term
`K J J^T x` from those rows (`contact_operator`, default 0). One row per
contact is what makes this affordable; storing the per-pair 3x3 blocks would
have been ~576 B per contact and dominated the matvec's bandwidth.

Frontend scenes, 900 substeps with the last 300 measured, mm per substep:

| Scene | model | off | on | per-step cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| two layers, 8 mm | planar 1 | 0.793 | 0.662 | 2.93 -> 3.28 ms |
| two layers, 8 mm | planar 0 | 1.55 | 1.23 | 3.18 -> 3.59 ms |
| single 3 mm | planar 1 | 0.609 | 0.570 | 2.67 -> 2.71 ms |
| single 3 mm | planar 0 | 3.06 | 2.77 | 3.97 -> 4.65 ms |
| flat single layer, 8 mm | planar 1 | 1.9e-6 | 3.7e-6 | 2.22 -> 2.23 ms |

So the coupling is real but removes only 6-21% of the motion: the limit cycle
has sources beyond the missing normal coupling.

On the real scene (study-1 garment plus body, `bench_rts.py`, 60 frames) the
option costs **RTS 0.466 -> 0.271**; with `friction_on=0` it runs at 0.213.
With friction on it also **fails outright**: `PCG ended with NaN residual`
after a few frames. The cause is a consistency bug in the emission - the
diagonal tiles the narrow phase already contributes include the Coulomb
friction stiffness, while the emitted rank-one row carries only the normal
penalty stiffness, so the operator's diagonal and off-diagonal no longer
describe the same term and the matrix can lose positive definiteness. The
capture also had to be treated explicitly: the row buffers are allocated
before the iteration loop and their identity/size is mixed into the CUDA-graph
key, otherwise the replayed graph would read freed memory (the first attempt
failed with `cudaErrorStreamCaptureUnsupported`).

Decision: ~50% of the frame for a <=20% jitter reduction, plus instability in
the default configuration, is not worth shipping. The engine changes were
reverted. If this is revisited, the friction part must be emitted consistently
(or dropped from the diagonal tiles) first.

### Follow-up: making the friction consistent, and what it costs

The friction term was then emitted consistently, in two steps:

1. **Rank-one slip-direction friction** (replacing the isotropic
   `scale*(I - n n^T)` in both the diagonal tiles and the emitted row). This is
   numerically *worse*: the per-vertex 3x3 block becomes `stiff*n n^T +
   scale*t t^T`, a rank-two matrix, and the Jacobi preconditioner inverts that
   block directly (`M_inv = diag.inverse()`), so it returns garbage along the
   direction orthogonal to both and the PCG fails.
2. **Isotropic friction kept in the diagonal, with two rank-one rows**
   (over the slip direction `t` and its bitangent `n x t`), so the diagonal and
   the emitted rows describe the same rank-two term and the 3x3 block stays
   full rank. This is the consistent version of the original formula.

With (2), on the two-layer stack, the 3 mm layer and the flat layer the
coupling now changes nothing measurable (0.789 vs 0.775 mm, 0.594 vs 0.613 mm,
both flat cases unchanged) at +2% to +24% per step, and on study-1 the RTS is
**0.398 -> 0.267** (-33%) and it still **fails with `PCG ended with NaN
residual`** on the second run. With `friction_on=0` the same build runs at RTS
0.280 (-30%), so the cost is the row count itself, not the friction rows.

Conclusion for option 3: the earlier, small jitter benefit came from the
*inconsistent* friction diagonal (extra tangential damping that the coupled
rows did not describe); once the operator is consistent the benefit disappears,
and the cost is a third of the frame rate plus a stability failure on the
garment scene. The engine changes were reverted again and are not shipped.
Whoever revisits this should start by making the contact matvec gather-based
(the current one pays four scattered atomics per row, which is what makes a
~40k-row contact set cost a third of the frame) and by keeping the emitted
term and the diagonal tiles provably identical.

**Make the contact hard instead of charged (projection replaces the penalty).**
Removing the penalty and enforcing non-penetration positionally is what a
position-based cloth solver would do, but it needs positional friction and a
velocity treatment as well (the penalty's friction disappears with it), and the
first attempt here showed the pile blowing up. It is a larger change than this
one and was not attempted.

**Raising `pd_iters` / `linear_iters`.** Measured to make the limit cycle
worse (0.31 -> 0.66 mm at 20 linear iterations on the folded-cloth scene) and it
is a user-facing quality knob, not a fix.

**Contact damping.** Measured ineffective in the previous round: the limit
cycle survives a 40x increase of the global velocity damping.
